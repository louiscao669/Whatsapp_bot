### What is this study about?

We are testing a new way to check whether a translation is good, for languages
where no expert is available to check it. The idea is simple: if a translation
has lost or changed meaning, people who read it will get more comprehension
questions wrong.

**We are testing the translation, not you.** Your answers are never scored as a
measure of your ability and are never shown to anyone outside the research team.

### Where do the passages come from?

The passages come from a Chinese translation of part of the Bible. All personal
and place names have been changed to invented names, so the passages will
probably not look familiar even if you know the Bible well. We do this so that
people who already know these stories cannot answer from memory instead of from
the translation in front of them.

**Please consider whether reading this kind of material is appropriate for your
own situation before you agree.** If you would rather not, simply close the page
— nothing happens, and no one is told.

This is not a religious activity. We will not ask what you believe, will not ask
you to accept anything, and will not send you any religious message. The questions are only about what each
passage says.

### What will I be asked to do?

1. **A short set of background questions (a few minutes).** About your language,
   your background, and how familiar you are with the Bible. **You may skip any
   question**, and you may skip the questions about religion entirely.
2. **A series of short reading sessions, spread over several weeks.** Each takes
   roughly 10–25 minutes. You open a link, read a passage, then answer questions
   about it — some multiple choice, some asking you to type a short answer of a
   few words.
3. You choose when to do each session. If you leave one unfinished, we will send
   you a reminder email. **You can turn reminders off at any time.**

In total this is about **two to three hours**, spread over several weeks. You may
ask us to send this information again at any point.

### Something important you should know

**Some of the passages we send you have been deliberately changed to contain
errors.** Words may be missing, replaced with the wrong word, or the grammar may
be wrong. We will not tell you which passages these are while the study is
running, because knowing would change how you read them — and that is exactly
what we are measuring.

This means **some passages will be hard or confusing to read. That is expected,
and it is not a problem with you.** Answer as best you can from what the passage
actually says. There is no penalty for a wrong answer, and your payment does not
depend on getting answers right.

When you finish, we will explain which passages were altered and how.

### How is my information handled?

**What we collect:** your email address, your background answers, your answers to
the comprehension questions, and when you opened and completed each session.

**What we do NOT collect:** your telephone number, your real name, your address,
your date of birth, your location, or your contacts. **You do not create an
account and never give us a password.**

**Your email address is the only thing that identifies you**, and we collect it
for exactly two purposes: to send you reminders, and to send you your payment.
It is stored separately from your answers, scrambled with a secret key kept
somewhere else, and deleted when the study ends. Your answers themselves are
stored under a random code number that means nothing outside this study.

**How we protect it:**

- Your email address is kept in a separate, restricted file from your answers.
- **That file is held by the faculty supervisor, Toby Jiajun Li — not by Louis
  Cao.** The person you know personally works only with code numbers and cannot
  tell which answers are yours, including your answers about religion.
- **Your access link is private — please do not forward or post it.** Anyone who
  has the link can open your session. It is the only thing needed to reach your
  part of the study.
- **We delete that file, and the secret key, within ⟨two months⟩ after the study
  finishes.** After that, nobody — including us — can trace an answer back to you.
- Nobody outside the research team is told whether you joined, whether you
  stopped, or how you answered. This includes anyone else who received the same
  invitation.
- Anything we publish reports group results only. No individual is described.

**Limits on confidentiality — please read this carefully:**

- Your answers are stored on **our own study servers, located in the United
  States**. We have built protections into the system, but no website can
  promise that a break-in is impossible.
- Your typed answers, with nothing identifying you attached, are sent to a
  **commercial automated language service in the United States** that compares
  them against a reference answer. That service does not use this text to train
  its systems. It may keep the text briefly — up to 30 days — to check for misuse
  of its service, after which it is deleted.
- **Reminder emails will appear in your inbox** and may be visible to anyone with
  access to your email. The emails contain no study content and no answers — only
  a prompt and your link. You may turn them off at any time.
- Visits to the study website may appear in **your own browser history** unless
  you clear it.
- ⟨Only if a leaderboard is enabled — otherwise delete:⟩ A handle you choose
  yourself may be visible to other participants on a participation leaderboard.
  It never shows your email, your real name, or how accurate your answers were,
  and you may turn it off at any time.

### What are the risks?

We do not expect this study to harm you. The main risks are:

- **Your information could be seen by someone who should not see it.** This is
  unlikely given the protections above, but no online study can promise it is
  impossible. What we hold includes what you told us about religion, which some
  people consider private.
- **The passages come from the Bible.** In some places, or in some families and
  communities, taking part in a study about such material could cause difficulty
  for you. **Please think about your own situation before agreeing.**
- **Some passages will be frustrating to read**, because they were deliberately
  damaged. You may skip any question and stop at any time.

develop a way to check translation quality in languages that currently have none.
