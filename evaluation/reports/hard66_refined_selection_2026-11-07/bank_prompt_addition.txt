Make two passes. First decide whether each stem earns its place as written. Read it aloud before checking the key. Set aside a stem that forces the reader to decode a generic or technical stand-in for a person, statement or event, even if the author’s intended answer is easy to recover. Set aside simple lookup when the rival is just another detail elsewhere in the window. A brief question can be strong when two participants take distinct roles in the same action, or when a proposal and its fulfillment have distinct consequences.

Then compare items within each passage. If two questions test the same inference in parallel scenes and changing the nouns would turn one into the other, favor the one that captures more meaning. Penalize the redundant item. Do not penalize independent items simply for sharing a question word or grammatical form.

Rank clear, independently worthwhile items ahead of items that require repair or add little coverage. In each reason, name the editorial decision that separates it from a weaker or stronger question; merely restating the key does not explain its value.
