Judge the question exactly as a participant would hear it. Identify the smallest piece of reasoning needed for a complete answer and check what a careless reader could actually confuse. Reserve high scores for a meaningful distinction that the stem asks naturally and the window resolves.

Give credit to short, plain questions when grammar assigns different roles or actions to nearby participants. Give credit when an indirect utterance has to be interpreted, or when the reader must distinguish what is proposed, authorized, underway or accomplished. A possible stylistic polish is a minor issue if the excerpt clearly resolves the intended meaning.

Lower the score when the apparent difficulty comes from translating an abstract label for a person, statement or event back into the passage. Also lower it when the key merely repeats an explicit fact or reason and the supposed rival has no plausible connection to the event. Check whether a later sentence gives away the answer without the interpretation the question appears to test.

Evaluate the proposed competitor against the actual wording. A weak supplied competitor alone does not disqualify a sound open question if a substantive inference remains; a nearby name alone does not establish a useful contrast. In the reason for the score, identify the decisive distinction or shortcut.
